How it works: Make a few choices, answer questions about your relationship, and receive a writing guide built around your answers. Fragments and bullet points are welcome. You do not need to know what to say before you begin.

Start with a few decisions.

This is your own workbook. If you and your partner want to follow a similar structure, discuss that together first. You will complete your questions and writing guide independently.

Step 1 · Length

How long do you want your vows to be?

Your answer determines how many questions you see and how much room the guide gives you to develop your ideas.

Step 2 · Your approach as a couple

How similar do you want your vows to be to your partner's?

You will write your vows independently. This choice is about whether you want to follow a shared structure.

A reminder: Talk with your partner about the approach you want. If you choose a shared structure, write down its name so you can tell them. You do not need to share answers or write vows together.
Step 3 · Your writing approach

Choose your writing approach.

Choose one shared writing structure

Your partner should choose the same structure separately if you want the same progression.

Choose the style or styles that feel like you

You can select more than one. Your partner can make their own choices.

Step 4 · Your answers

Tell us what matters to you.

Your answers are raw material for your writing guide, not finished vows. Write in your own words, use bullet points, and leave anything blank if it does not apply.

You can add as many topics as you like. Each topic adds its available questions to your questionnaire.

Step 5 · Your writing guide

Your own path to writing your vows.

This is not a set of finished vows. It is a collection of your answers, reminders, writing exercises, and places to draft each section yourself.

Before you finish: Read your draft aloud. Keep details that could only come from your relationship. You do not need to include every answer. Make promises that are meaningful and realistic. Your first draft does not need to be perfect.
